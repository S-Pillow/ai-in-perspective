**State:** Implemented on a review branch only. Full CI/build, browser behavior, accessibility and owner visual acceptance must be checked before release. No merge or publication is authorized by this research/implementation unit.

## Owner direction
Build one substantial new Environment chapter, borrowing the explanatory depth and selectable-topic pattern of AI & Global Competition. Keep six topics: household bills/grid upgrades; electricity and emissions; corporate transparency/accountability; cumulative hyperscale buildout with a plain-language definition; benefits versus local costs; alternatives to hyperscale expansion. Use native Dig deeper disclosures, substantive prose, and an honest visual comparison of efficiency and total electricity use. User-provided articles and infographic screenshots are research leads and design inspiration, not mandatory or accepted sources.

## Source and design decision
Use \`src/components/EnvironmentCommunity.astro\` for the content/semantic illustrations, \`src/styles/environment-community.css\` for visual identity responsive to Environment's existing design, and \`src/scripts/environment-community.js\` for optional progressive enhancement. Insert after the existing bigger-picture/context section and before practical responses. No changes to established energy or water diagrams, Global Competition, News collection, quotas, tracking, site configuration, routes or deployment.

One-site/cluster SVG is explicitly conceptual and makes no quantitative claims. The efficiency figure's 10+5 and 100+20 electricity-unit arithmetic is hypothetical: same scale, lower relative overhead but higher total consumption. PUE measures total facility energy / IT energy; it does not measure work accomplished or permit blanket real-world comparisons. No fixed hyperscale square footage, server count, GPU/CPU exclusion, average water use or projected facility size is asserted.

## Source register, checked Nov 23 2026
- Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, **United States Data Center Energy Usage Report: 2025 Update**, published June 2026, https://eta.lbl.gov/publications/united-states-data-center-energy-2025 . National 2030 share scenarios 9.5% to 15.3%; reference 11.8%; all data centers, not AI-only. 2024 U.S. 4.7% estimated figure independently cross-checked in Sep 2026 Congressional Research Service R49326.
- Congressional Research Service, **Data Centers and the Electricity Grid: Frequently Asked Questions**, September 1 2026, https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R49326.html . Electricity costs, mix of studies, load uncertainty, regional grid, state authority. Link is to a report mirror, not the official CRS site.
- U.S. Department of Energy, **Electricity Rate Designs for Large Loads**, January 17 2025, https://www.energy.gov/policy/articles/electricity-rate-designs-large-loads-evolving-practices-and-opportunities . Fair cost allocation, stranded investment, capacity and tariff design.
- International Energy Agency, **Energy and AI**, April 2025, https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary . Global and local scale, clustering, emissions/supply scenarios, flexibility and siting.
- International Energy Agency 4E, **Policies for Data Centre Energy Efficiency**, 2023, https://www.iea-4e.org/wp-content/uploads/publications/2023/02/DC-Workstream-activity-1-publication-version.pdf . Hyperscale has no universally formal definition; data-center size alone is not an adequate efficiency claim.
- ASHRAE, **Data Centers and Telecommunication Facilities**, https://handbook.ashrae.org/handbooks/A19/SI/A19_Ch20/A19_Ch20_si.aspx . PUE definition, strong caution on cross-center PUE comparisons.
- Virginia Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission, **Data Centers in Virginia**, Dec 2024, https://jlarc.virginia.gov/landing-2024-data-centers-in-virginia.asp . Local taxes, construction benefits, rate impacts, demand, noise, water, zoning and regulation. Treat state findings as place-specific.
- Georgia Tech / Daniel Yue and Yiyang Zeng, **The Local Economic Effects of Data Center Entry**, 2026 discussion https://news.gatech.edu/news/2026/07/10/why-better-cities-and-towns-see-more-benefits-data-centers-rural-regions . Metro/rural benefit heterogeneity and utility-territory limitations. Observational research, not a universal causal prediction.
- MIT CEEPR, **Who Pays for Growth? Evidence from Data Centers and the Grid**, 2026 working paper, https://ceepr.mit.edu/workingpaper/who-pays-for-growth-evidence-from-datacenters-and-the-grid/ . Data-center entry associated with retail-rate increases across its studied utility sample. Supports a nuanced rate discussion; observational estimates cannot predict a given local utility.
- User-supplied Nautilus, Cato, Medium, NCRC articles were considered as perspective/discovery inputs, but are not mandatory and are not relied on for site numerical claims.
