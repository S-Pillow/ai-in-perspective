What changes?

Access to useful tools, the ability to deploy them, and whether businesses can afford to compete.

Who is affected?

ANOTHER IMPORTANT QUESTION

America is spending heavily on AI. Is it paying off yet?

There is not one answer because money invested, revenue earned, company profits and value delivered to users are four different things.

Investment

Capital is being committed to companies and infrastructure in the hope of future returns. It is not the same as profit.

Revenue

AI companies are making money from customers, subscriptions and services. Revenue can grow even while costs grow faster.

Profitability

Major AI developers can have large revenue and still spend more than they earn. The financial picture differs by company.

Value to users

Companies and people may benefit from AI even when the model developer is not yet profitable.

A real example: Reuters reported in September 2026 that OpenAI projected substantial spending on computing through 2030, alongside rapid revenue growth. That helps explain why large AI investment has not meant immediate profits for every developer. Read the Reuters reporting.

05 / The other possible future

What could American
AI leadership make possible?

Continued development doesn't guarantee leadership. But if the U.S. remains competitive,or becomes a leader across more of the AI ecosystem,the possible opportunities reach far beyond chatbots.

Build here

American teams create models, software, services and production methods.

Defend here

Security and public institutions can investigate and use advanced tools responsibly.

Discover here

Scientists and medical researchers can participate in the next generation of breakthroughs.

Leadership is one possible result, not a promise. A country can remain competitive without leading every category. Continuing AI development also brings genuine safety, employment, environmental, and governance challenges.

06 / Keep both sides in view

Neither future
comes without tradeoffs.

Explore the possible costs of restriction and the risks of continued development. These are different types of consequences, not a numerical scorecard.

A broad U.S. ban

What might it protect against?

  • Some domestic deployment risks and harms from poorly tested systems
  • Some misuse of restricted tools within U.S. jurisdictions
  • Some pressures from premature adoption, surveillance, or workplace automation

What might it put at risk?

  • Domestic innovation and the ability to compete internationally
  • American access to developing defensive and scientific capabilities
  • Reduced dependence on foreign technology providers

Continued U.S. development

What might it enable?

  • New tools and businesses, and the possibility of continued global competitiveness
  • AI-supported science, medicine, and cybersecurity
  • More domestic capacity to inspect, shape, and govern critical AI systems

What risks remain?

  • Job disruption, privacy abuses, concentrated power, and AI-assisted attacks
  • Unreliable systems in high-stakes settings and potentially large infrastructure demands
  • The possibility that other countries still innovate or deploy more effectively

This thought experiment contrasts a broad U.S. ban with continued development. Targeted safeguards, licensing rules, restrictions on specific uses, and international agreements are different policy choices that can change these tradeoffs.

The everyday explanation

Explain Like I'm Five:

Four simple ideas about what could happen if America bans AI while other countries keep using it.

01 / The world

Other countries would keep going.

America can make rules for America. But those rules would not stop people in other countries from building and using AI. American businesses would still have to compete with them.

02 / Jobs and businesses

Falling behind could cost us work.

AI can help teams write software, design products, and finish tasks faster. If companies overseas can use those tools but American companies cannot, they may win customers and jobs that might otherwise stay here.

03 / Keeping people safe

Threats would not stop at the border.

Cybercriminals could keep using AI to help carry out attacks. American security teams might have fewer ways to spot trouble and respond quickly, making banks, hospitals, utilities, and other services harder to protect.

04 / Discoveries and new ideas

Important progress could happen elsewhere.

Scientists use AI to search through information and find promising ideas to test. If American researchers cannot use similar tools, discoveries and opportunities in medicine, science, and technology could move ahead in other countries.

Protecting people from AI's risks matters. So does thinking about what America could lose by stepping away while the rest of the world moves forward.

The question to leave with

Could trying to avoid AI's risks create different risks for America?

You don't have to know the future,or support unrestricted AI,to examine that possibility. The question is what happens to American workers, businesses, security, and discoveries if the U.S. stops while others continue.
